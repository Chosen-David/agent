The compatible mechanism is a small, verifiable chain: source method and figure intent → explicit critical components/relations and permissible abstraction → editable layout → rendered figure → independent semantic and final-size visual checks → caption and prose handoff. Existing `research-diagrams_execution.md` already requires nodes/edges/directions/grouping and source-plus-render checks; `research-figures_execution.md` already requires final assembled-size review. These papers motivate executing and testing those requirements. Repeating them in longer prompts is not itself a candidate improvement.

## AutoFigure — reasoning and rendering must be checked separately

Identity: Minjun Zhu, Zhen Lin, Yixuan Weng, Panzhong Lu, Qiujie Xie, Yifan Wei, Sifan Liu, Qiyao Sun, Yue Zhang. *AutoFigure: Generating and Refining Publication-Ready Scientific Illustrations*. arXiv:2602.03828v1, submitted 2026-02-03 (PDF labels ICLR 2026). Version v2 exists; claims here are pinned to v1.

Read §§2–6, physical pp3–10; Appendix B/C/D pp15–16, case/failure text p18 and F–P pp19–27, and selected figure captions. Visual inspection: pp9,10,18 (Fig5 ablations, Fig6 RLHF, Fig9 Waterfall). Not read: systematic introduction pp1–2, full Appendix E p17, case gallery Q pp28–38, bibliography; no upstream run.

Method/evidence: §3 distinguishes 300 test samples (200 papers, 100 other sources) from 3,000 development samples. §4 distills entities and relations into SVG/HTML, maintains a best-scored layout, then renders and replaces recognized text. §5.1/Table2 reports paper-category overall 7.03 and 53% pairwise win rate against human references; this is evaluator-defined preference, not 53% factual accuracy. §5.2 recruits 10 authors evaluating figures for 21 papers. Fig4's 83.3% is ranking-derived across competing models; it is not a win rate versus the original alone. Appendix B.2 says ranking cards expose model names, so that expert ranking is not blind.

Important negative evidence: Fig5a/b shows Claude content fidelity 7.35→6.61 and Gemini 6.97→6.54 after rendering, contradicting a blanket interpretation that aesthetics never compromise fidelity. Table7 full versus no text refinement: overall 7.18 versus 7.14, accuracy 7.45 versus 7.53. No uncertainty is supplied to establish these small deltas as significant. Appendix F explicitly reports surviving character errors, speculative concretization, and wrong hierarchy in underspecified input. The RLHF example's body text contains misspellings visible in Fig6; a declared text-correction stage is not a perfection guarantee.

Costs/limits: Appendix J/Table8 reports ~17.5 min/$0.20 with Gemini API, ~9.3 min with local Qwen on H100 hardware; the latter excludes hardware and electricity. Appendix M's worked rendering uses Nano-Banana plus ClipDrop, whereas main evaluation names GPT-Image, and Table8 describes local rendering/OCR without enough detail to verify equivalent end-to-end rendering costs. Do not transfer the zero-cost claim or assume hosted availability. Appendix H's near absence of both-bad comparisons against a human reference does not establish absolute quality of the generated candidate: one good reference alone prevents both-bad.

Candidate mapping: DG-01. Compare identical source, figure scope and budget with/without a post-polish semantic snapshot check. Preserve all iterations and inspect every required endpoint/label; do not select by the generator's aggregate score alone. No need to implement OCR erasure or adopt the paper's cartoon aesthetic.

## PaperBanana — style gains can hide scientific loss

Identity: Dawei Zhu, Rui Meng, Yale Song, Xiyu Wei, Sujian Li, Tomas Pfister, Jinsung Yoon. *PaperBanana: Automating Academic Illustration for AI Scientists*. arXiv:2601.23265v1, submitted 2026-01-30 (PDF cover date 2026-02-02). Version v2 exists; analysis pins v1.

Read §§2–9 pp3–12; Appendix A failures/statistical plots pp19–20, B/C/D pp21–23; G methodology critic pp38–39, plot critic continuation p43; H evaluation prompts pp44–49. Visually inspected pp19–20 (Figs9–10). Exclusions: introductory pp1–2, references, other case gallery pages, long style/retrieval/planner prompt listings and full plot prompts; no code or model run.

Method/evidence: text/caption determine communicative scope; reference retrieval prioritizes diagram structure; style guidelines then alter a planned description; critic checks rendered output against source. Statistical plots instead use executable Matplotlib for numerical precision. Dataset filtering restricts methodology aspect ratios to 1.5–2.5, rejects simplistic/cluttered/abstract designs, then splits 584 curated diagrams in half. These design restrictions limit claims about all scientific figure styles.

Results: Table1 overall 60.2 versus vanilla Nano-Banana-Pro 43.2, but faithfulness remains 45.8 versus human reference anchor 50. Table2 row4→row3 adding stylist without critic changes faithfulness 39.2→30.7, conciseness 61.7→79.2, aesthetics 67.4→72.1. Row3→row1 with three critic iterations recovers faithfulness to 45.8. Random versus semantic retrieval without style/critic is similar (overall 48.3 versus 49.2), weakening any need for expensive domain-exact retrieval. Differences are reported score points, not correctness percentages or a reproduced causal generalization.

Evaluation limits: source/context/caption plus reference support pairwise judgments; Appendix H permits semantic abstraction yet forbids fabricated modules, bypasses and wrong direction. Human/VLM Kendall tau overall is .45 on 50 cases. Two annotators resolve disagreements by discussion; three judges perform a separate 50-case blind A/B preference study. Both-good and both-bad both map to 50, making their numeric aggregate insufficient for acceptance. Appendix C acknowledges multiple candidates were manually selected for illustrations presented in the paper; do not infer single-shot reliability from showcase figures. No latency, token or dollar measurements were found in this fixed paper.

Failure inspection: Fig10 explicitly shows wrong attention sources, absent action→state connections, and an illegal tactic→decoder shortcut. These are materially wrong while polished. Fig9 shows a radar value reversal, duplicated category and wrong bar height from image generation. Claims for statistical plots therefore require data/code checks, including sparse plots; do not adopt the suggested sparse-raster compromise where exact numbers matter.

Candidates: DG-01 tests style revision semantic preservation; DG-02 uses source-based hard correctness gates with independent visual preference secondary. Reject copying automatic legend removal, universal background/rectangularity vetoes or a ten-reference requirement; these are paper-specific design policies, not proven necessities.

## SciFlow-Bench — rendered topology is useful, inferred truth is fallible

Identity: Tong Zhang, Honglin Lin, Zhou Liu, Chong Chen, Wentao Zhang. *SciFlow-Bench: Evaluating Structure-Aware Scientific Diagram Generation via Inverse Parsing*. arXiv:2602.09809v1, 2026-02-10. Later v2/v3 exist; all values here are v1.

Read §§1–6 pp1–8; limitations/ethics p9 before references; Appendices A–D pp11–17. Visually inspected pp7,15,16 (Table3, ambiguity examples Fig5, generation comparison Fig6). Bibliography pp9–10 not systematically read. No implementation run; no all-page visual audit.

Mechanism: planning creates shared visual prompts; segmentation and OCR separately recover entities; fusion resolves identities; Mermaid intermediate encodes observed relations; final graph comparison is based on semantic node matching and directed edges. Dataset has 500 figures from 2025 papers; 100 are reviewed by trained annotators editing the automatic output. Table1 parser/reference reliability is node-F1 .91, edge-F1 .71. Table4 ablation full parser is node-F1 .92/edge-F1 .64; removing OCR drops node recall .96→.48 and edge-F1 .64→.24. These are parser diagnostics, separate from generator scores and with a different full-parser edge value than Table1; do not silently conflate them.

Table3 gives Gemini-3-Pro graph/image/overall .116/.535/.311; Graphviz .091/.463/.283. The metric gap motivates examining arrows independently but cannot be read as a percentage of factually correct figures because these composites have different definitions/scales. Hard examples score higher than easy for the strongest models; the paper attributes possible influence to richer text. This is not a controlled scaling causal experiment.

Audit concerns: Appendix A.1 allows a transitive path to count as a correct edge, tolerating missing intermediates; source-semantic matching is asymmetric and permits multiple predictions per reference. This may be appropriate abstraction but cannot certify preservation of a mandatory step. The human protocol starts with automatic identities and minimal correction, so errors shared by parser and reviewer can remain. Only 100/500 were human checked. The paper calls the pipeline deterministic but does not supply empirical repeatability or named parser snapshots and numeric similarity threshold in the read implementation appendix. Appendix A.2 says GPT-4o is not used as a generator, while §5.1 explicitly uses GPT-4o to generate Graphviz DOT. This weakens its blanket evaluator-independence statement for that baseline.

Costs/limits: p9 explicitly limits coverage to framework-style directed diagrams and excludes fine aesthetics. No quantitative inference cost/latency or equal-cost comparison located. OCR failures, symbolic nodes, dashed/implicit links and dense crossings are shown in Appendix C. A figure can have several legitimate graph abstractions, so compare source-backed required relations plus explicit allowable compression, not one unreviewed inferred graph.

Candidate DG-03: independent reconstruction/check of rendered critical connections against a curated method specification. Controls should include a wrong endpoint, reversed arrow, omitted mandatory intermediate, duplicated label, and an allowed abbreviation/layout change. Reject treating total score or automatic graph as ground truth. Report parser disagreement as unresolved inspection, not generator failure until localized.

## SciFig — editable composition still needs component-level checks

Identity: Siyuan Huang, Yifan Zhou, Yutong Gao, Zi Yin, Juyang Bai, Xinxin Liu, Rama Chellappa, Chun Pong Lau, Cheng Peng, Sayan Nag, Shraman Pramanick. *SciFig: Towards Automating Editable Figure Generation for Scientific Papers*. arXiv:2601.04390v3, 2026-06-26. This is the substantially revised editable system; do not combine its numbers with v1's differently titled system.

Read main §§1–6 and limitations/ethics pp1–10 (before references); Appendices A–F pp13–22 including component planning, refinement, QA prompts and heuristics. Visually inspected pp14,16 (author/generated comparison; refinement and two failure examples). Not systematically read: bibliography, all visuals at individual component resolution; no external tools execution.

Method: a plan determines components, hierarchy and flow; layout agent makes XML; component agent chooses PNG assets or native text; feedback edits locally. Harness checks overlap, labels and changes, routes formulas to Matplotlib and graphs to Graphviz (App C.3). Benchmark curation starts with 5,570 2023 AI/ML papers, filters then human-verifies 2,219 with two reviewers plus adjudication; 435 test and 1,784 rubric examples are separated. C&C uses 50 paper-specific yes/no questions; a different set of models answers only from figure, while general rubric uses 20 shared questions. Reference and perceptual metrics are separate axes, not a single score.

Evidence: Table1 C&C 7.5 versus PaperBanana 7.1; rubric average 7.7 versus 7.3. Table3 baseline average 5.5, +layout 6.0, +feedback 6.5, four refinement rounds 7.7; this additive sequence is not a matched-compute isolation of each mechanism. Table4 reports 10,000 human comparisons from 60 participants; note table omits Qwen-Image despite appendix saying nine candidates. §5.2 gives ~10 minutes and $1.2 including evaluation per figure, ~525 dollars for 435; later limitation wording calls this generation cost. Preserve that cost-scope ambiguity rather than calculate a repository budget from it.

Limits: original reference C&C is only 6.4, while generated outputs score higher; automatic question/reference perception is not perfect and extra detail is not necessarily better communication. App D.3 requires questions answerable from rendered figure but examples include fixed spatial arrangements, which can penalize a valid new layout. Equal yes/no accuracy does not establish coverage or handle all-yes bias; calibrate with known correct/incorrect controls. Appendix E heuristics include canvas occupancy, uniform whitespace and bounding-box non-overlap, which can reward simplistic output and mistake intentional grouping for clutter; Paper2Poster scores 9.2 perceptual quality but 2.3 C&C. No single heuristic should decide publication suitability.

Most consequential failure: App B/FigB.1 shows a missing node and wrong MRR/cosine formula within PNG components although outer layout is good. Thus component placement editability is not internal vector editability or mathematical correctness. The system is benchmarked only on methodology figures in AI/ML; statistical plots/teasers are qualitative examples.

Candidates DG-01/DG-03 use local edit preservation and component checks; DG-02 borrows independent questions about essential source facts, without inheriting a 50-question quota or learned judge as oracle.

## Minimal repository experiment proposals (all unexecuted)

- **DG-01 — semantic invariants through refinement** (T25; research-diagrams / figures). Three distinct real-paper cases, with source methods, original diagrams and explicit figure purpose fixed before generation. Independently record essential modules, allowed grouping/abbreviation, relation endpoints/direction/type, losses and variables. Generate baseline, preserve it, apply a bounded style/layout refinement, then compare both editable semantics and actual rendered pixels. Pass requires zero changed/omitted critical scientific facts, acceptable final-size legibility and preserved source/editability. Record each failure and repair; a visual preference gain alone cannot pass. Candidate is **test**, not a claim that extra artifact schemas are needed.
- **DG-02 — correctness and design remain separate** (T20/T21/T25; evaluator and figure→writer handoff). **Adopt as experiment design**, pending actual task acceptance: reviewers first validate specified facts/constraints, then judge readability and design with blinded order. Preserve both-good, both-bad and uncertain outcomes separately. Render at the target manuscript size; verify caption scope, notation and surrounding prose. Existing semantic/visual gates already imply this; no blanket prompt expansion proposed.
- **DG-03 — challenge the actual rendered-figure checker** (T21/T25; diagrams / independent review). **Test** the scoring path on matched original/candidate artifacts plus intentionally altered copies: one reversed edge, one illegal shortcut, one missing required intermediate, one wrong formula or label, and one equivalent relayout. Require zero false acceptance on critical corruptions and acceptance of permissible relayout, with reviewer localization and source citation. The source XML alone must not decide the raster result; parser uncertainty requires independent inspection. These controls assess evaluator validity, not an estimated real-world model error rate.

T25 still requires actual generation of three real cases, source/original comparison, independent semantic and visual review, final-size viewing, preserved failed attempts/repairs, and figure/prose handoff. This research does not complete those requirements. Main AI owns ledger/TASK updates and final acceptance.
