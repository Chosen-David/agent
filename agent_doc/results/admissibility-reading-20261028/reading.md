## Method and evidence

Admissibility conjoins scope, policy and query-relative lifecycle with three-valued established/unresolved labels, independently of relevance. Historical queries can need superseded source facts. Trusted namespace is authenticated provenance, not inferred topic. Rank non-worsening requires flat support, complete trusted labels, required anchors inside it and unchanged within-namespace scores; not a safety theorem for graph selectors or incorrect labels.

The trace distinguishes stored IDs, retrieved IDs, prompt exposure and target disclosure. Verifier abstention differs from unresolved evaluator labels. Matched-required-recall prefixes charge every preceding item; report feasibility, known-label coverage, lower/upper bounds and violation counts. Positive irrelevant insertions can dilute fractions, hence any-violation/count companions. Infeasible routes get unit penalty in a composite comparative loss, not a conditional contamination probability. No-anchor queries are recall-unevaluable; undefined denominators remain unavailable. Source-macro products need not reproduce the mean product.

Experiments are separate populations:3767held-out RHELM/MemOps queries,72analysis cases from96development cases (24calibration),1523paired native reader cases,16controlled scenarios/192pairs per reader, plus separate GateMem observational data. Public group splits and trusted namespaces constrain representativeness; RHELM reader effects use only seven groups. Route settings selected among165development candidates at top100; main top20 admissibility reanalysis changes scoring from legacy usability to eligibility without retuning rankings. Corrected v2 released-field attribution differs from historical v1; do not merge them or call all analyses preregistered.

Controls include ten-seed random size-matching, deep global postfilter, anchor-preserving oracle, fixed feasible-set comparison, metadata corruption, policy omission, thresholds and cluster routing. Random partitions harm recall; deep postfilter can recover support but retains global score work. The oracle uses gold and is not deployable. At top20 only31.1%namespace queries reach80%required recall;87%of composite loss improvement comes from feasibility. Removing scope makes remaining upper risk slightly higher under namespace (.126 vs .123); policy exposure increases. Thus support success does not prove all eligibility axes improved.

Frozen native route-to-reader accuracy gains are observational because recall also changes. Labels are released-field operational references: human repeatability for prohibited status is weak (alpha.0829) despite high raw agreement; lifecycle moderate. Policy-omission sensitivity preserves the principal support direction, not policy-label validity. Text verifiers over-deny required anchors: no tested low-denial threshold both meets the1%constraint and detects violations. Some metadata gates raise Gemini stale disclosure; more filtering is not uniformly beneficial. Missing/corrupted metadata failure brackets are tested grid points, not deployment tolerances.

Controlled stateless exposure pairs differ in focal candidate presence, with unique literal markers and interleaving. Markers inside refusals still count, so literal disclosure is distinct from useful answers/internal causal use. Only one of four reader-specific relevant-inadmissible intervals excludes zero;16scenarios are not population leakage prevalence, general safety or a model ranking. Native answer non-response is not gold-action-aware over-refusal. Pointwise paired namespace/scenario bootstraps respect each study's grouping; readers/populations are not pooled. Shared judge/200-output audit and sequential Luna replication remain limitations. GateMem G3 equalsG2 because no pre-query delete/supersede operations occur in that release; its predictive exposure association is not randomized evidence.
