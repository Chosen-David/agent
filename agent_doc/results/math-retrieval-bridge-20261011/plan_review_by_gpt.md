The interface provides the intended bridge, with an important limitation: `search` examines only `summary`, `run_id`, `result_id`, `producer_task_id`, and `context`. It does **not** search the referenced advice or navigation contents. The historical summaries therefore need accurate task-language terms for compression, prediction, whole-task failure, and device alarm trajectories. Adding hash references alone cannot establish discovery.

The implementation also distinguishes `show` from artifact verification: `show` checks record structure and returns its hash, but does not rehash every historical artifact. The planned explicit reference-hash check is therefore necessary and appropriate.

The before-search `partial: true` flag must remain accurately qualified. In this implementation it can indicate either bounded enumeration or omitted hits beyond the requested result limit. It does not establish that all historical material was examined or that no relevant record exists anywhere.

For execution and final review, retain these concrete acceptance boundaries:

- Preserve identical queries and search parameters before and after registration, and report the actual returned ranks and IDs.
- Treat an absent or low-ranked target as a discovery limitation; do not silently retune queries and describe the result as an unseen retrieval success.
- Bind the original advice, navigation, report, and complete review bytes by their actual hashes. Preserve both original result directories’ existing files.
- Keep historical context fields absent when unknown; do not invent experimental code, configuration, metrics, or measurement time.
- Freeze all references for the new MATH-80 history record before registration. Later changes to a referenced artifact would make that immutable registration stale.
- Keep path-event refusal explicit: marginal epsilon/rho bounds alone do not certify “ever failed,” cumulative permission compliance, or an alarm sequence.
- Append the MATH-80 index/progress evidence through the designated single writer and preserve unresolved host and MATH65 states.

The deferred trusted-host gates are real operational blockers for managed dispatch and scientific adoption. They do not prevent this explicitly bounded ordinary document repair. Final approval of the produced documents should depend on their actual records, query outputs, hashes, and preservation evidence; this plan review does not pre-accept those future results.
